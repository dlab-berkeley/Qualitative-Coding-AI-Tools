# What did we actually learn?

Not whether AI is good or bad.

Whether we can see what it did.


---

## The model gave us output

It gave us codes.  
It gave us quotations.  
It gave us explanations.  
It gave us ambiguity notes.

That is not the same thing as giving us findings.



---

## Three different problems

A quotation can be inaccurate.

A quotation can be real, but attached to the wrong code.

A passage can support more than one defensible reading.

Those are not the same problem.



---

## The codebook is part of the system

If the code is vague, the model will use the vagueness.

So will humans.

A better prompt cannot fully fix an unstable codebook.



---

## What AI was useful for today

It made disagreements visible.

It applied the same codebook quickly.

It produced evidence we could inspect.

It forced us to ask where our definitions were too loose.



---

## What AI did not replace

It did not decide what matters.

It did not know the ethical stakes of the material.

It did not make the source unnecessary.

It did not repair the codebook for us.



---

## A workflow you can take home

Small corpus.  
Human coding.  
Draft codebook.  
AI first pass.  
Evidence audit.  
Codebook revision.  
Test again.



---

## Before using this on your own data

Ask:

Can this material be pasted into the tool?

Would I be comfortable documenting this workflow in a methods section?

Can I check the evidence without trusting the model?

What would I do if the model disagrees with me?


---

## Closing question

What would you trust this tool to code?

And what would you never hand it?
