They were housed on the second floor of the farm equipment barn. It was pretty good size barn; it was about eighty feet wide by about a hundred and twenty feet long. They were bunked at what they would call the hayloft. They got an army cot or an army stretcher, the thing that the Red Cross used, stretchers, where they carried off the wounded. They were about five inches off the ground. They didn't get a mattress; they got blankets, no mattresses. They slept on Army surplus blankets. They had their own little stoves. They had a stove for about every five or six guys. It was a kerosene stove that they used. The boards of the floor, which was the floor of the loft, were not tight up against each other, they had maybe about a half-inch or three-quarter inch gap between boards. Naturally, you get a natural draft up through the boards, and at night, it got cold, it got miserable cold, until some of them got the idea of putting cardboards down. I told Spence that they were cold, that they were complaining that it was too cold, and he went and got them a big huge heater. It wasn't warm, warm, you know, like you could wear a tee shirt, but it was comfortable warm. Better than nothing at all, a lot better.
