**Slide 2 — Why this room, why this corpus**

**Slide 3 — Agenda**

**Slide 4 — What a bracero oral history is, in one minute**
Layout: image left (archive photo if rights allow), text right.
Content: introduce the historical grounding — the Bracero Program, 1942 to 1964, guest-worker agreement between the US and Mexico.

**Slide 5 — Meet the corpus**
Layout: big number, small caption, repeated across the slide.
Content: 32 passages, all English-language, split by stratum — bracero-and-family, grower/official, labor intermediary.

**Slide 6 — The codebook, tier one**
Layout: five-card grid, one card per T1 code, code name + one-line definition each.
Content: T1-CROP, T1-SITE, T1-CONTRACT, T1-PROC, T1-WAGE.

**Slide 7 — The codebook, tier two**
Layout: same five-card grid, different color treatment to signal the shift from factual to interpretive.
Content: T2-AMBIV, T2-DIGNITY, T2-NEGCOMP, T2-RETRO, T2-HARDSHIP.
Timing: 4 min.

**Slide 8 — The one rule that matters**
Layout: single sentence, huge type, centered.
Content: "Every code needs a verbatim quote. No quote, no code."
Timing: 1 min, but let it sit for a beat before advancing.

**Slide 9 — Exercise 1: hand-code, no AI**
Layout: instructions as a numbered checklist, timer graphic if available.
Content: six passage handouts, codebook in hand, 12 minutes, work alone first then compare with a neighbor for the last 4. The six:
  - **bha_1593_024** (Max Render) — dual recordkeeping by race. Clean T1-PROC anchor; almost everyone codes this the same way, which is the point — it's the control passage that shows the codebook works before things get harder.
  - **bha_1561_001** (Arnulfo Pompa) — company-store deduction leaves a $2 paycheck. T1-WAGE plus T2-HARDSHIP.
  - **bha_1574_002** (Raymundo Villa) — flatly denies any deductions ever happened. Hand this out right after 1561_001, don't announce the contradiction — let someone notice it out loud.
  - **bha_1592_012** (Marion Haynes) — braceros buying Singer sewing machines to send home. Genuinely splits between T2-DIGNITY and T2-AMBIV; expect real disagreement here.
  - **bha_1573_004** (George Veytia) — sheriff deputizes braceros into a gas-well fire, company disclaims responsibility. Heaviest passage in the set; T2-NEGCOMP.
  - **bha_1588_003** (Wonda Lou Wonicar) — short, merchants profiting off arrivals, "mixed feelings." Good closer: quick to code, resets pace after the Veytia passage.
Timing: 16 min total.

**Slide 10 — Debrief: where did you disagree?**
Layout: open, mostly whitespace, maybe a single prompt line.
Content: "Raise your hand if you and your neighbor coded the same passage differently." 
Timing: 6 min discussion.

**Slide 11 — Now write T2-HARDSHIP yourselves**
Layout: text-only, the code name alone at the top, blank space below for a live-built definition (projected notes app or whiteboard, not baked into the deck).
Content: none pre-written. This is the codebook-repair moment the whole session builds toward.
Timing: 5 min.

**Slide 12 — Transition: watch it answer itself twice**
Layout: simple diagram — one chat-window icon, an arrow to a second identical icon.
Content: one line explaining what's about to happen: same passage, same prompt, two separate browser tabs, no shared memory between them.
Timing: 1 min.

**Slide 13 — Live demo: same prompt, two tabs**
Layout: full-screen share placeholder, no deck content of its own.
Content: facilitator opens Chat Tab 1, pastes the codebook plus **bha_1574_003** (Raymundo Villa's trust arc, "Sí patronsito" to real confidence over three seasons — short, warm, genuinely undecided between T2-DIGNITY and T2-AMBIV), sends it, reads the answer aloud. Switches to a blank Tab 2, pastes the exact same prompt, sends it, reads that answer aloud. Asks the room: same codes? same evidence spans? what changed?
"The same model, the same prompt, can give different answers on different runs. That's not something you did wrong. It means one run is never proof of reliability — 'it worked once' is not 'it works.' This is exactly why we always ask for a verbatim evidence span: confident-sounding text is cheap, an exact quote that's actually in the passage is not."
Timing: 8 min.

**Slide 14 — Governance pause (do not skip, do not shorten)**
Layout: text-only, plain background, no decoration — this should visually read as different in tone from everything around it.
Content, read close to verbatim at a normal pace, about one minute:
  > "You are about to see this applied to real oral history material, and you just watched it done live. Before we go further: know your own institution's policy on pasting research data — including material that's public-domain or unrestricted-use — into a consumer AI chat tool. The corpus we're using here is checked as Unrestricted use, held by UTEP's Institute of Oral History. Your own data may not carry the same permissions. A session like this one sends you home able to paste anything into a chat window. That ability comes with the responsibility to check first."
Timing: 3 min.

**Slide 15 — The pre-computed sheet: all 32, already coded**
Layout: bar chart or small-multiples grid, T1 codes on one side, T2 codes on the other, agreement rate across the sheet.
Content: results from Codex's production-side run against the full 32-passage corpus, built before the session — not generated live. Hand it out (or reveal it) and say almost nothing at first.
Timing: 14 min (includes quiet reading time).

**Slide 16 — Exercise 2: catch the fabrication**

Timing: 8 min.

**Slide 17 — Why this matters beyond a classroom exercise**
Layout: text-only, single block.
Content: a short paragraph connecting fabricated evidence in a teaching exercise to the same failure mode in any real research or archival application of these tools.
Timing: 3 min.

**Slide 18 — Finish the T2-HARDSHIP repair**

Content: none pre-written; this is the payoff of slide 11's opening.
