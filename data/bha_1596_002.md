I was first introduced to La Migra at an early age. I was about 8 years old, 7 years old. And we're doing our thing, chopping the weeds out of the cotton. And one of the men screamed "La Migra." And everybody, out of that 15 or 20 workforce with the exception of me and my older sister and some of the other teens, we spoke English. We look around like, why is everybody running. And these guys dropped their tools, and they are literally running as fast as they can because off in the distance you could see the olive green vans in a trail of dust coming. And it didn't dawn on me until later, when the big bus came — they were making an immigrant bust, an illegal immigration bust. And I got picked up. It took one hour for me to convince these people that, yes, I was born in Mexico. I do have papers; I just don't have them with me. My dad — it just so happened that Cucko, the band guy... he came over and said, "No. This is — this young man belongs to Gilgardo Pompa who works for Goodyear Farms who is here legally. So you have no right to take this young man." And honestly, for a few minutes I thought, man, I'm going back to Mexico, and I don't know what I'm gonna do when I get there.
